MSc Thesis Work

More Than Supervision: Accountability-Driven Coach Agent for Mitigating Academic Procrastination

An HCI research project translating real accountability coaching practices into an LLM-based conversational agent, then evaluating the prototype through a mixed-method user study.

24 stakeholder interviews: 12 coaches and 12 clients
N = 36 4-week randomized mixed-method evaluation
17.71% within-group PASS reduction in the AI coach condition

Research Questions

How do accountability coaches and clients describe the interaction mechanisms that support academic self-regulation?

How can those mechanisms be translated into a stateful LLM-driven conversational coach?

Human-Centered Research Pipeline

1. Fieldwork Semi-structured interviews with real accountability coaches and clients.
2. Pattern Distillation Accountability Coaching Service Pattern with foundational, operational, and regulatory layers.
3. System Prototype Central coordinator, sub-agents, shared memory, intent routing, and adaptive response aggregation.
4. Evaluation 4-week randomized mixed-method study with quantitative PASS outcomes and qualitative interviews.

System Architecture

Intent Routing Maps user turns into task, planning, tracking, support, or resource actions.
Shared State Maintains goals, tasks, check-ins, emotional signals, and supervision preferences.
Response Policy Aggregates module outputs into coherent coaching responses.
Central Coordinator Synchronizes the full coaching loop across planning, task tracking, guidance, knowledge support, emotional support, and supervision customization.
Schedule Planning Priority sorting, Pomodoro blocks, load adaptation.
Task Tracking Reminders, Da Ka check-ins, progress signals.
Support Modules Task guidance, knowledge support, emotional support.

Interaction Mechanisms

needs alignment trust building adaptive reminders emotion-sensitive load reflection pre-commitment reward context restructuring knowledge grounding

Accountability Coaching Service Pattern

Foundational Needs alignment, boundaries, coaching intensity, and trust calibration before intervention.
Operational Schedule planning, task tracking, guidance, knowledge support, and emotional support across the task lifecycle.
Regulatory Role and tone adaptation based on progress, emotion, task difficulty, and user response.

Evaluation Results

AI coach PASS reduction
17.71%
ANCOVA effect
p < .001
Effect size
eta_p^2 .504

One-way ANCOVA showed a significant intervention effect on post-test PASS, F(2,32) = 16.23, p < .001. Compared with no intervention, the AI coach produced a lower adjusted post-test PASS score, mean difference -6.08, 95% CI [-8.67, -3.48].

Qualitative Finding

Participants experienced the AI coach as judgment-free, highly available, and standardized, which helped reduce social-evaluation anxiety and supported more honest check-ins.

Design Contributions

From practice to pattern Distilled real coaching interactions into a structured service pattern that can guide system design.
From pattern to prototype Implemented a modular conversational agent with memory, routing, adaptation, and coordinated support.
From prototype to evidence Evaluated the system with quantitative outcomes and qualitative accounts of interaction experience.
